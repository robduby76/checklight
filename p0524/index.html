P0524: Engine Oil Pressure Too Low (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0524 means the engine computer is reading oil pressure that's too low. That might be a real loss of oil pressure, which can wreck an engine quickly, or it might be a bad sensor or wiring. Both sources say to treat it as serious: stop, check the oil level and condition first, and get the actual pressure tested before blaming the sensor.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0524 means

The oil pressure sensor (or sender) measures the engine's mechanical oil pressure and reports it to the powertrain control module (PCM) as a voltage. P0524 sets when the PCM sees too low a value from that sensor. On many vehicles the red oil pressure warning light comes on at the same time. OBD-Codes.com describes it as a generic code for 1996-and-newer vehicles that shows up more often on Dodge, Jeep, Chrysler and Ram, and lists P0520, P0521, P0522 and P0523 as related codes. Unlike P0520, which is a sensor circuit fault, P0524 says the pressure itself reads low. The sources lean a little differently on the likely cause: OBD-Codes.com starts with oil level, viscosity and contamination and says a mechanical gauge test should come before condemning the sensor, while RepairPal's list of common problems leads with sensor failure but names an oil and filter change and oil pump replacement as the common repairs. OBD-Codes.com also mentions a Chrysler/Jeep/Ram/Dodge bulletin (18-008-08) where the fix is reprogramming the PCM, and a Mazda bulletin (R088/12) that may involve replacing the oil pump.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

RepairPal rates P0524 urgent and warns that continuing to drive with it set may result in engine failure. OBD-Codes.com calls it severe and says that if you notice low oil pressure you should pull over and shut the engine off as soon as it's safe. Don't keep driving to get home: check the oil level with the engine off, and if you can't confirm the engine has oil and real oil pressure, have it towed.

Common symptoms

  • Oil pressure warning light on (both sources)
  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Oil pressure gauge reading low or zero (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Abnormal noise from the engine (both sources)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Low oil level or actual low oil pressure (OBD-Codes.com)
  2. Wrong oil viscosity, or oil contaminated with fuel or coolant (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Faulty engine oil pressure sensor (both sources)
  4. Wiring issue, such as the sensor signal shorted to ground (both sources)
  5. Oil pump failure or worn internal engine parts like bearings (both sources)
  6. Less often: PCM failure (RepairPal), or a known software issue on some Chrysler-group vehicles fixed by reprogramming (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. With the engine off and the car on level ground, check the oil level and condition on the dipstick. OBD-Codes.com calls this the absolute first step: the oil should be full and not look contaminated.
  2. Think back over the maintenance history. OBD-Codes.com says skipped oil changes or the wrong viscosity make worn internal parts, and real low pressure, more likely.
  3. Look up technical service bulletins for your vehicle. OBD-Codes.com names Chrysler/Jeep/Ram/Dodge bulletin 18-008-08 and Mazda bulletin R088/12.
  4. Inspect the wiring and connector at the oil pressure sensor and toward the PCM for broken or frayed wires, burnt spots or loose pins (OBD-Codes.com). A repair manual shows where the sensor is on your engine.

Typical fixes

  • Top up the oil, or change the oil and filter with the correct viscosity if it's wrong or contaminated (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal lists oil and filter change as a common repair)
  • Repair damaged wiring or a bad connector in the sensor circuit (both sources)
  • Replace a faulty oil pressure sensor after testing confirms actual pressure is normal (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace the oil pump or repair internal engine wear if actual pressure is low (RepairPal lists oil pump replacement as a common repair)
  • Apply a PCM reprogram if a bulletin covers your vehicle (OBD-Codes.com)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com says the ideal next step is a mechanical oil pressure gauge test; if real pressure is low, the problem is inside the engine and the sensor and wiring aren't the issue
  • Testing the sensor and wiring against factory specs takes a digital volt-ohm meter (OBD-Codes.com)
  • RepairPal cautions that just replacing a sensor may not fix the underlying problem, so get a proper diagnosis

FAQs

Can I drive with P0524?

It's not a good idea. RepairPal rates it urgent and says driving with it may result in engine failure, and OBD-Codes.com says to pull over and shut off the engine as soon as it's safe. Check the oil level, and have the car towed if you can't confirm it has oil and real oil pressure.

Is P0524 just a bad sensor?

Sometimes, but don't assume it. OBD-Codes.com says to check the oil level and condition first and then test actual pressure with a mechanical gauge. If real pressure is low, the problem is inside the engine, such as a worn oil pump or bearings.

What's the difference between P0524 and P0520?

P0520 is a problem with the oil pressure sensor's circuit or signal. P0524 means the sensor is reporting oil pressure that's too low, which may be a real and serious lubrication problem.
