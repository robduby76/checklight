P0456: Evaporative Emission (EVAP) System Leak Detected (very small leak) (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0456 means the PCM found a very small leak in the EVAP system, which stores fuel vapor so it doesn't escape. The most common cause is a loose or faulty gas cap, so start there.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0456 means

The EVAP system captures fuel vapor from the tank in a charcoal canister and later purges it into the engine to be burned. On enhanced EVAP systems the PCM periodically closes the canister vent valve to seal the system, then watches the fuel tank pressure (FTP) sensor to see whether it holds pressure or vacuum. RepairPal notes this self-test usually runs after the engine is shut down. P0456 means the test found a leak too small to set the larger-leak codes. The sources word it slightly differently: RepairPal's definition says "very small leak," while OBD-Codes.com's title says "small leak detected" but places it alongside P0455 (large leak) and P0457 (fuel cap) in the same EVAP family.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Usually OK to drive

Generally safe to keep driving: OBD-Codes.com says you likely won't notice anything except the check engine light. RepairPal rates it moderate, so get it diagnosed within a reasonable time, and sooner if you smell fuel.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Usually no other noticeable symptoms, because the EVAP system only handles fuel tank vapor, not engine management (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Loose, incorrect or faulty gas cap (both sources list this; OBD-Codes.com calls it the usual cause)
  2. Small leak in an EVAP hose or fuel tank hose
  3. Purge valve or vent valve not sealing
  4. Leaking EVAP (charcoal) canister (listed by OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Refueling with the engine running, or not tightening the cap fully (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check the gas cap: make sure it's the correct cap, tighten it fully, and look for a cracked or worn seal. RepairPal says caps are inexpensive, so replace it if you have any doubt and clear the code.
  2. Turn the engine off before refueling.
  3. Look over the EVAP hoses you can reach for cracks, breaks or loose connections.
  4. Don't try to find the leak by pressurizing the EVAP system with compressed air: OBD-Codes.com warns this can damage the purge and vent solenoids.

Typical fixes

  • Tighten or replace the gas cap
  • Replace a cracked or disconnected EVAP or fuel tank hose
  • Replace a leaking purge valve, vent valve or EVAP canister found by testing

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • A very small leak is hard to find by eye. RepairPal says that without the proper equipment it can be nearly impossible, and recommends an OEM-level scan tool plus a smoke machine.
  • A shop can command the vent solenoid closed with a scan tool and watch the FTP sensor reading: a steady reading means the system seals, a drifting one means a leak (OBD-Codes.com). A smoke test then shows smoke escaping at the leaking part.
  • Checking manufacturer technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle, as RepairPal suggests before a full step-by-step diagnosis.

FAQs

Will P0456 clear by itself after I tighten the gas cap?

RepairPal says most codes disappear on their own once the system no longer detects the problem. Because the EVAP leak test only runs under certain conditions, it can take several drives before the light goes out.

How is P0456 different from P0455 and P0457?

All three are EVAP leak codes. P0456 is a very small leak, P0455 is a large leak, and P0457 points to the fuel cap being loose or off. Diagnosis starts the same way for each: gas cap first, then hoses, then a smoke test.
