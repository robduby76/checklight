P0441: EVAP System Incorrect Purge Flow (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0441 means the PCM saw the wrong amount of purge flow in the EVAP system, the part that sends stored fuel vapor into the engine to be burned. The purge valve (solenoid) and its wiring are the usual suspects, but the gas cap and hoses are worth checking first.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0441 means

The EVAP system stores fuel vapor from the tank in a charcoal canister. When the engine is running, a purge valve opens and intake vacuum pulls those vapors into the engine to be burned. P0441 is a generic code meaning that purge flow isn't what the PCM expected. The sources describe the trigger differently. OBD-Codes.com says a vacuum switch detects purge flow, and the code sets when the PCM commands purge but the switch shows no flow. RepairPal says the code sets when the PCM detects flow when it should not, and its technician notes add that during the leak test the vacuum can be too high for too long (a purge valve stuck open) or too low or missing (a purge valve stuck closed, a leak, or a bad fuel tank pressure sensor). In short, both too little and too much purge flow can lead to P0441, depending on the vehicle.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Usually OK to drive

Usually safe to keep driving: OBD-Codes.com says you likely won't notice anything other than the check engine light. RepairPal rates it moderate, so get it diagnosed within a reasonable time, and sooner if you smell fuel or the engine idles rough or stalls.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Usually no other symptoms (both sources)
  • Sometimes a fuel smell from escaping fuel vapor (RepairPal)
  • In some cases a rough idle, hesitation or stalling (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty purge valve (solenoid), including one stuck open or stuck closed (both sources)
  2. Broken, damaged or restricted EVAP lines or charcoal canister (both sources)
  3. Wiring faults: an open in the PCM purge command circuit, an open or short in the purge solenoid's voltage feed, or corrosion or resistance in the purge connector (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. A bad vacuum switch (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Missing, damaged or poorly sealing gas cap, a damaged filler neck, or a defective vent valve (RepairPal)
  6. A leaking fuel tank sending-unit gasket or a damaged fuel tank (RepairPal)
  7. Rarely, a bad PCM (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check that the gas cap is present and tight, and that its seal isn't dry or cracked. RepairPal lists the cap as a common cause and a common misdiagnosis, so don't stop there if the code returns.
  2. Look over the EVAP hoses and the purge solenoid connector you can reach for cracks, disconnected lines or corrosion.
  3. Clear the code and drive normally for a few days to see if it comes back.

Typical fixes

  • Replace a faulty purge valve (solenoid) or vent valve found by testing
  • Repair damaged or restricted EVAP lines or a damaged charcoal canister
  • Repair wiring or connector faults in the purge solenoid circuit
  • On some Chrysler vehicles, replacing the leak detection pump (LDP), which OBD-Codes.com calls a common Chrysler fix
  • Reseat or replace the gas cap if it won't seal

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Testing the purge and vent valves to confirm they open and close on command and hold vacuum for at least 30 to 60 seconds (RepairPal).
  • Watching fuel tank pressure readings on a scan tool, since the PCM relies on the fuel tank pressure sensor for the EVAP test (RepairPal).
  • Checking the purge solenoid's voltage feed, the PCM command circuit and the connector for opens, shorts or resistance (OBD-Codes.com).
  • A smoke test of the whole EVAP system to find leaks hidden behind or under components (RepairPal).

FAQs

Will P0441 go away on its own?

RepairPal says most codes disappear once the onboard system no longer detects the issue. If the code stays or keeps coming back, get it diagnosed, because a sticking purge valve usually won't fix itself.

Can a gas cap cause P0441?

RepairPal lists a missing or damaged fuel cap among the common causes, but also lists the cap as a common misdiagnosis. OBD-Codes.com doesn't list the cap at all and focuses on the purge solenoid, vacuum switch, lines and wiring. Check the cap first because it's cheap, but expect the purge valve or its circuit to be the more likely culprit.
